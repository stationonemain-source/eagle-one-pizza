01What we found

Your online ordering switches off on 30 April 2027.

Every one of the 14 “Order Online” buttons on the site opens GloriaFood, the free ordering system from Oracle. GloriaFood’s own homepage now says the service “will reach end of life on April 30, 2027”, and that Oracle does not offer a direct replacement. That is seven months from today. When it goes, the buttons at all seven stores stop taking orders, and the menu, customer list and order history inside it go too unless they are exported first. It works today, and your reviewers name the online pickup discount as a reason they order. That is exactly why the move needs planning rather than a scramble in April.

The menu and every price live inside two picture flyers, and they disagree.

The Our Menu page lists toppings and specialty pizzas with no prices. The prices are in two flyer images (JPEGs) further down, which Google, AI assistants and screen readers cannot read. The page says “All Prices Reflect Carry Out Only” above a list with no prices, then “Please Call” with no number after it. A large one-topping pizza is $6.99 in one flyer, $10.99 in the other, and $8.99 in the SW OKC ordering menu. The ordering page advertises a “10% off pick-up special” directly above a “35% off pizza” pick-up special. The page text lists a stuffed crust; the flyer lists cauliflower.

On a phone it is a shrunken 2012 desktop page.

None of the 8 pages carries a mobile viewport tag, so a phone lays each one out 990 pixels wide and shrinks it: body text lands at about 5 pixels tall. Lighthouse mobile scores speed at 57 (largest paint 5.4 s) and best practices at 31. The homepage’s “Call us” link opens the ordering page instead of the phone. The only tap-to-call links are on the Directions page, and three of those seven have an invisible character typed in front of the number.

The newest photo on the site was taken in June 2012.

The Gallery holds 11 photos, each 333 pixels wide. Ten are pizzas in your boxes, with the camera’s date stamp still printed in the corner: 06/15/2012 and 06/20/2012. There is no photo of any of the seven stores, the staff, the gyros or the cheese sticks your reviewers keep praising. The Announcements page still announces the Lexington grand opening, under a graphic that says Holdenville.

6,027 Google reviews, and the website never mentions one.

Your seven Google listings hold 6,027 reviews, rated 4.3 to 4.6, and for “pizza” in their own town six of the seven are Google’s first map result. That is the strongest local reputation in this scan by a distance. But all seven listings send people to the same homepage rather than to their store, six of them through the old http address. Lexington is filed as “Restaurant”, not “Pizza restaurant”. The Norman listing carries a review about a nail salon. And the 2023 Readers’ Choice award from The Oklahoman appears only inside a flyer image. The complaints that do appear in 2026 are about the handoff: missing items, cold delivery, and one online order charged and then cancelled.

Nothing measures anything, and has not since 2023.

The only analytics tag on the site is Universal Analytics (UA-7870337-1). Google stopped processing standard Universal Analytics on 1 July 2023, so no visit to the site has been counted for over three years. There is no ad pixel and no email or text list, so the thousands of people who order online every month cannot be invited back. The homepage also still loads a review badge from Urbanspoon, a service that closed; the browser blocks it as insecure.

06Three fixes for this week
1

Export everything out of GloriaFood now. The menu, the customer list and the order history, for all seven stores, while support is still answering. Then choose the replacement well before spring, so April 2027 is a non-event.

2

Fix the Google listings in twenty minutes. Change Lexington’s category to Pizza restaurant, switch all seven website links to https, report the nail-salon review on the Norman listing, and add fresh photos of each store. None of it costs anything.

3

Turn counting back on. A free Google Analytics 4 tag takes ten minutes and replaces the one that stopped in 2023. Without it you can’t know how many orders start on the website.
